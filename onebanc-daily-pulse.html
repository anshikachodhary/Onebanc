OneBanc · Dashboard Assignment

Daily Pulse — a dashboard that follows your day, not your login.

Three states of the OneBanc home screen, each tuned to a different hour and a different question, built around a single recurring module called the Daily Pulse.

Approach & Assumptions

My starting point was that a banking app shouldn't feel the same at 9 AM as it does at 5 PM, because the person opening it isn't in the same place either. At 9 AM, OneBanc helps Meera set up her day — a soft spending outlook based on her typical Tuesday, plus what's due. By 2 PM, after a few UPI payments, the app shifts into tracking mode — a live read on pace, and the one transaction that nudged her off her usual rhythm. At 5 PM it becomes a wind-down screen — a clean recap that credits her for a good day and quietly sets tomorrow's baseline. I assumed a working professional in her late 20s who pays for most things through UPI in small amounts, checks her phone often, but has never actively "budgeted" in the traditional sense.

Why It Changes, & Why Users Return

The three screens shift along one axis — time direction — starting with what's coming, moving to what's happening, ending with what happened. That alone gives each screen a distinct job: forecast, track, review. Nothing is padded with meaningless variety; every card earns its place because it answers what she'd actually be wondering at that hour. The reason to come back isn't a streak counter or a push notification — it's that the app's one answer is only valid for a couple of hours before it needs refreshing, the same way checking the weather again makes sense once the sky's actually changed.
